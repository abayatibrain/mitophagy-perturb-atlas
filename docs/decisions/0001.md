# ADR-0001: Keep DepMap and Perturb-Seq in separate similarity spaces
Date: 2026-05-18
Status: Proposed — awaiting Armin sign-off

## Context
DepMap (cancer-line CRISPR fitness) and Replogle 2022 (Perturb-Seq in K562
and RPE1) measure different things. Both can be used to compute
gene-gene perturbation similarity. The question is whether the atlas
should join them into a single embedding ("a gene is similar to PRKN if
it is close in joint space") or report similarity in each modality
separately.

## Options considered
- **Option A: Separate spaces.** Two parallel similarity tables; the UI
  shows both.
  - Pros: each signal stays interpretable; no need to defend a fusion
    weighting; sign of effect preserved.
  - Cons: more output to scan; users have to look at both.
- **Option B: Joint embedding (e.g., CCA, MOFA+).** A single space.
  - Pros: one ranked list.
  - Cons: requires defending the alignment of two very different signals;
    risks spurious convergence; the weighting choice itself becomes a
    free parameter that nobody can defend without a paper-length argument.
- **Option C: Show only one (DepMap OR Replogle).**
  - Pros: simple.
  - Cons: throws away half the signal.

## Criteria for choosing
- Interpretability beats convenience.
- The atlas's value is showing convergence (and divergence) across
  modalities — joining the spaces destroys that view.
- The README must be defensible to a biology Principal who has not read
  our integration paper (we don't have one).

## Decision
**Option A.** Keep the spaces separate. Render side-by-side rankings.
Phenocopy candidates that appear in *both* spaces are flagged with a
high-confidence badge in the UI.

## Consequences
- Two similarity computation paths to maintain.
- The empirical null for FDR is computed per modality.
- Convergence across modalities becomes a feature, not an afterthought.

## Open questions for Armin
- Confirm Option A, or override to Option B with a fusion ADR.
- Confirm DepMap release pinning at 24Q2 (or specify a different release).
